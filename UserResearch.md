# User Research

## What we wanted to know
Why university students who do want to read can't manage to do it regularly, and what the AI they use to study has to do with it.

## How we researched it
1. Desk research (published studies and surveys).
2. Google Forms survey for university students, answered on 17 November 2026. Responses: 6. Shared through: whatsapp chats. The raw export is in [`research/survey.csv`](research/survey.csv) and the questions are in the appendix.
3. Short interviews (about 3-5 min each) with the same 6 students who answered the survey, aged 21 to 24. Date and format (in person or remote): 17 November 2026, all in person individually. We took notes by hand and quote them below with spelling and capitalization lightly corrected.

All participants are anonymous and took part in a class project. We refer to them as P1 to P6 in both the survey and the interviews.

## What we found

From the sources
- 55 % of university students who use AI say it makes them think less deeply (Inside Higher Ed, 2025) and 67 % of students believe it harms their critical thinking (RAND, 2026).
- In Mexico, people who don't read say it is because of lack of interest (34.6 %) or lack of time (32.4 %) (INEGI, MOLEC 2025).
- A habit takes 66 days on average to form, though it varies a lot between people, and missing one day doesn't break it (Lally et al., 2010).
- Answering questions about what you read helps you remember it more than rereading (Roediger and Karpicke, 2006).
- On average, people understood texts better on paper than on a screen, mostly for informational texts and under time pressure (Delgado et al., 2018).

From the survey (6 responses)
- **They want to read, but they don't.** 5 of 6 want to read more books than they do now, and 4 of 6 read two books or fewer for fun in the last year.
- **Lack of time and the phone are the main barriers.** Lack of time was chosen by 4 of 6 and phone and social media by 3 of 6. Lack of interest was chosen by 2 of 6 (P5, P6), and tiredness and cost or access to books by 1 each (P1). Everyone named at least one of time or phone.
- **The phone is present while they read.** 5 of 6 check it at least sometimes while reading (2 often: P2, P4).
- **Most people pick a habit back up after missing a day.** 5 of 6 pick it up the next day, 1 of 6 (P2) feels guilty and stops for a while, and nobody quits completely.
- **They prefer paper, mostly because of how it feels.** 4 of 6 prefer paper, 2 have no preference, and nobody prefers screen. Reasons: "Because I like the feeling of the paper" (P1), "Feels more natural" (P4), "I love the feeling" (P5), "I am used to paper" (P6).
- **Opinions on AI are mild.** On the "AI makes me think less deeply" statement, 2 of 6 answered 4 or 5 (P3, P4), 4 of 6 answered 3, and nobody answered 1 or 2 (average 3.5).

What the interviews showed:
- **The phone is within reach, and messages are the reason.** 3 of 6 (P1, P3, P5) said the phone is nearby or distracts them while they read, and 2 of them (P1, P5) pointed to messages, not social media. P3 gets distracted often but still finishes the book.
- **People who like reading get absorbed, and time is what stops them.** P3 and P4 enjoy reading, and P4 read less this year because of lack of time. This matches the survey, where 4 of 6 chose lack of time.
- **Getting the book is a small but real friction.** P1 finds good prices for thick books annoying and P3 orders from Amazon. P1 also chose cost or access in the survey.
- **Our participants did not see AI as a problem.** 3 of 6 (P2, P5, P6) use AI to study, and described it as "a tool" (P2) or "very good" (P5). Nobody expressed worry about it.
- **Missing a habit can discourage people for a while, but they come back.** P2 said a few weeks off from the gym "turned me down for a while" and then returned. This was about the gym, not reading, and only one person mentioned it.
- **Some are open to trying it.** 2 of 6 (P1, P4) said they would try or use an app like this. P1 wants to try it before committing.
- **Paper can be about comfort.** P6 would pick paper because screens make their eyes tired.

## Behaviors and constraints
- **Behaviors:** they keep the phone close or check it while reading (5 of 6 in the survey at least sometimes, 3 of 6 in interviews), they prefer paper (4 of 6), and after a missed day most resume the next day (5 of 6).
- **Constraints:** lack of time (4 of 6 in the survey, P4 in the interviews), tiredness (1 of 6), and cost or access to books (P1 in the survey and the interviews, P3 buys on Amazon).

The "I already ruined it" thought we first wrote for the Relapse stage did not come up in any interview, so we replaced it with what P2 said. The thoughts for Check in and Quiz are still assumptions from the product idea and the sources.

## How this changed the app
| Decision | Based on | Type of evidence | Where in the app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading momentum instead of a streak | Missing a day doesn't break a habit (Lally). Survey: 5 of 6 resume the next day. Interview: P2 was discouraged after weeks off | Theory, with support from our survey and one interview | `HabitEngine.cs` |
| The app asks you to close it, and Focus mode with the apps you choose | Survey: 5 of 6 check the phone while reading. Interviews: P1, P3 and P5, with messages as the trigger for P1 and P5 | Our survey and interviews | `ClosingScreen`, Focus mode |
| Small goal and a 10 s check-in | Lack of time is the top barrier (4 of 6, and P4) | Our survey and interviews | `SetupScreen`, `LogSheet` |
| Try it without an account | P1 would try it but isn't sure they would stick to it | Our interviews (one participant) and Fogg | `WelcomeScreen`, `AccountService.cs` |
| "Buy it" with real prices for printed books | P1 finds good prices for thick books annoying (and chose cost or access in the survey), P3 orders online, 4 of 6 prefer paper | Our survey and interviews | `Shop` in `MainScreens.cs` |
| Chapter quizzes | Answering questions helps you remember (Roediger and Karpicke) | Theory only | `QuizScreen` |
| AI doesn't write anything for the user | Inside Higher Ed and RAND. Our participants did not confirm a worry: 2 of 6 agree AI makes them think less, 4 are neutral, 3 use it and see it as useful | Theory. A design stance, not something our participants asked for | `ReflectionScreen`, "Written by a human" |

Decisions marked "Theory" came from published research, not from our participants. What we changed after testing the app with people is in [UserTesting.md](UserTesting.md).

## Limits
- Small convenience sample (6 people), mostly students of engineering, design and architecture, with nobody aged 18 to 20.
- The interviews were short (3-5 min) and our notes are brief, so we do not claim to cover every stage of the journey. Check in, Quiz and Come back are still partly assumptions.
- The same 6 people answered the survey and the interviews. Their answers differ a little: P1 answered "never" to checking the phone in the survey but keeps it nearby in the interview, and P3 answered "sometimes" but described being distracted "often".
- P2's example of missing a habit was about the gym, not reading.
- P6 does not want to read more, so they are outside our target group.
- The survey did not record the university and did not ask about book prices, remembering what they read, or whether they use AI. P1 chose three barriers although the question asked for up to two, so barrier counts are mentions.

## Appendix: survey questions
1. Age
2. Degree (what you're studying)
3. Do you want to read more books than you do now? (Yes / Not sure / No)
4. How many books did you read for fun in the last 12 months? (0 / 1-2 / 3-5 / 6 or more)
5. What most often stops you from reading? (choose up to 2: phone and social media, lack of time, tiredness, lack of interest, cost or access to books, other)
6. When you read, how often do you check your phone? (Never / Sometimes / Often / Constantly)
7. When you miss a day of a habit (reading, gym, studying), what do you usually do? (Pick it up the next day / Feel guilty and stop for a while / Quit it completely)
8. How much do you agree: "Using AI to summarize or study makes me think less deeply." (1-5)
9. For a book you want to understand well, do you prefer paper or screen? (Paper / Screen / No preference)
10. Why? (optional)

Interview questions:
1. Last time: “Tell me about the last time you tried to read a book. What happened?”
2. Feelings: “How did you feel while reading? And when you stopped?”
3. Phone: “What happens with your phone when you’re reading?”
4. Missed days: “Think of a habit you dropped, like the gym or studying. What happened the day you skipped it? What did you think?”
5. Getting books: “How do you usually get the books you read? Is price or finding the right copy ever a problem?”
6. AI: “Do you use AI for study or reading? How do you feel about it?”
7. Reaction: “If an app helped you read on paper by keeping your phone quiet and asking you to close it afterwards, what would you think?”
